\section*{Experiment details}

\paragraph{Benchmark and partitions.}
We evaluate on all 119 tasks of SWE-bench Science \cite{xu2026swebenchscience}: 20 scientific
domains, 98 repositories, mostly Python with C, C++, Fortran, MATLAB and Cython tasks in the
remainder. Thirty tasks were used for development and iteration; the remaining 89 were locked
before the reported runs and form the only inferential partition. Development runs four
replicates; the held-out partition runs three.

\paragraph{Arms.}
Both arms run the same agent with the same tools except for the intervention.
The \emph{baseline} arm has ordinary shell tools only. The \emph{CBE} arm additionally queries a
prepared evidence store through three endpoints: \texttt{science\_find} (targets and source
excerpts), \texttt{science\_inspect} (relationships, definitions, source, contracts) and
\texttt{science\_note} (an optional record that never gates repair).

\paragraph{Preparation.}
Preparation runs once per task and makes no model call and executes no code. Files are ranked so
that execution evidence and boundary callers precede shallow entry points. Python is parsed with
Tree-sitter and C, C++, Fortran and Cython with Joern code property graphs, with explicit fallback
when a frontend is unavailable. The store records computations with their result anchors,
conditions and findings with source spans, documented public definitions, and call boundaries
classified as internal, external with a named provider, or \texttt{unknown\_external}. Across the
94 tasks whose prepared bundles are frozen, a task yields a median of 296 scientific objects,
with a maximum of 10{,}887; the boundaries recorded during held-out runs comprise 12{,}794
external, 1{,}890 unresolved and 534 internal calls, of which \texttt{numpy} accounts for 1{,}554.

\paragraph{Execution conditions.}
Model: DeepSeek V4.1 Flash \cite{deepseekusage} at temperature $0$ with \texttt{PYTHONHASHSEED=0};
the provider ignores the seed parameter, which was verified separately. Agent budget 1800\,s per
attempt, verifier budget 1800\,s. Neither container has network access. The verifier runs only
after the repair stops, so private tests are never visible to either arm. A solve is the official
verifier reward; `verified` counts attempts that produced a verdict, and attempts lost to provider
or container failures are excluded rather than counted as failures.

\paragraph{Outcome measures.}
Primary: official verifier solve rate per attempt, compared per task with a paired sign test and a
bootstrap interval over per-task rate differences. Secondary: output and input tokens, agent
wall-clock per attempt, private-test pass fraction as partial credit, and the number of replicate
runs that solved each task.

\paragraph{Headline numbers.}
Held out ($k{=}3$, 522 verified attempts): baseline 60/262 (22.9\%), CBE 60/260 (23.1\%);
74 tasks tie, 7 favour CBE and 8 favour the baseline; sign test $p = 1.00$, bootstrap 95\,\%
interval $[-3.4, +4.9]$\,pp. Output tokens 12.01M against 10.36M, a 13.7\,\% reduction, i.e.\
45.8k against 39.8k per attempt. Private tests 2315/3167 (73.1\,\%) against 2226/3148 (70.7\,\%).
Development ($k{=}4$): baseline 43/117 (36.8\%), CBE 44/118 (37.3\%).

\paragraph{Discipline split (held out).}
Mechanics $+22.2$\,pp (0/9 $\rightarrow$ 2/9), astronomy $+12.4$\,pp (2/14 $\rightarrow$ 4/15),
materials science and engineering $+9.7$\,pp (8/44 $\rightarrow$ 12/43), civil engineering
$+6.7$\,pp, atmospheric science $+1.4$\,pp; biomedical engineering $-16.7$\,pp
(13/30 $\rightarrow$ 8/30) and biology $-9.5$\,pp (7/18 $\rightarrow$ 5/17); chemistry (19 tasks),
physics (7) and mathematics (5) unchanged. Development: mathematics $+37.5$\,pp
(0/8 $\rightarrow$ 3/8), statistics $+25$\,pp, chemistry $-10$\,pp.

\paragraph{Replication.}
Per arm, 60 (baseline) and 61 (CBE) held-out tasks are never solved; 11 and 10 are solved by every
replicate; 18 flip inside an arm; the arms disagree on 15 tasks in total. The joint distribution is
dominated by 55 tasks at 0/0 and 10 at 3/3.

\paragraph{Reasoning effort.}
On the development tasks the baseline leads at high effort (12/30 against 11/30) and CBE leads at
low effort (13/30 against 12/30). On the 20-task subset evaluated at both levels, the high-effort
CBE deficit (8/20 against 10/20) closes at low effort (10/20 against 10/20), and the two tasks
recovered under low effort are the two CBE lost at high effort. Tool use in that subset: 61
\texttt{science\_inspect} calls, 10 \texttt{science\_find} calls and 20 records over 20 tasks.

\paragraph{Discordant tasks (held out).}
CBE gains on 020, 042, 067, 074, 084, 103 and 116; it loses on 022, 029, 053, 056, 063, 087, 093
and 098. Task 103 (pyNastran; stiffness matrices of a symmetric composite laminate) is solved
twice in three replicates with CBE and never by the baseline; task 022 (nilearn; volume-to-surface
projection) is solved twice by the baseline and never with CBE.
